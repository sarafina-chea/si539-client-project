Preparing for Your Experience

Before Your Experience Begins

Engaged learning gives UMSI students the opportunity to apply classroom knowledge to real challenges. Before beginning a project, it is important to understand the community, establish expectations, and prepare to collaborate with others.

Unlike a traditional classroom assignment, an engaged learning project involves real people, organizations, and changing circumstances. Preparation helps students approach this work with realistic goals, clear responsibilities, and respect for their project partners.

What to Consider Before You Begin

Effective preparation involves understanding yourself, your team, and the organization or community you will be working with.

Understand Your Role

Consider how your identity, experiences, assumptions, and perspectives may influence the way you approach a project.

Rather than entering a community as an outside expert with all the answers, approach the work as a collaborative learner. Listen carefully, ask questions, and recognize the knowledge your partners already have.

Learn About Your Community Partner

Research the organization, its mission, the people it serves, and the context surrounding the project.

Think about who may be affected by your work and how community members and organizational partners should participate in decisions.

Assess Your Team's Readiness

Discuss each team member's skills, interests, availability, responsibilities, and learning goals.

Identify any gaps in your team's knowledge and consider what additional support or preparation may be necessary.

Establish Project Expectations

Review project commitments, deadlines, leadership responsibilities, and expected outcomes before beginning the work.

Start identifying the project's scope, potential constraints, and questions that need to be discussed with your partner.

Your Preparation Checklist

Use these steps to organize your preparation before starting an engaged learning project.

  1. Reflect on your perspective: Consider how your experiences, assumptions, and identity may shape your approach to the project.
  2. Research the project context: Learn about the organization, community, project history, and affected stakeholders.
  3. Set learning goals: Identify the technical, professional, interpersonal, and ethical skills you hope to develop.
  4. Review your team's capacity: Compare your team's skills and availability with the needs of the project.
  5. Clarify expectations: Review deadlines, responsibilities, commitments, and intended outcomes.
  6. Identify open questions: Write down what you need to learn from your partner before finalizing the project's scope and deliverables.

Questions to Ask Yourself

Before beginning your project, consider the following questions:

  • What do I hope to learn from this experience?
  • Who will be affected by the project and its outcomes?
  • What assumptions am I making about the partner, community, or project?
  • What knowledge and skills does my team already have?
  • What knowledge or perspectives are missing from our team?
  • How will the partner participate in project decisions?
  • What would a mutually beneficial outcome look like?

Helpful Preparation Resources

These resources can help you reflect on your responsibilities, understand community engagement, and organize your project.

Self-Reflection and Community Engagement

  • Social Identity Snapshot - A tool for reflecting on social identity, self-awareness, and positionality.
  • "Check Yourself" Community Engagement Checklist - A checklist for considering personal assumptions and ethical readiness.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation - A framework for understanding different levels of public involvement and decision-making.

Project Planning and Scoping

  • Project Scoping Handout and Terms - Guidance for translating partner needs into realistic project goals.
  • Project Management Worksheet - A worksheet for organizing project objectives, team roles, and timelines.

Leadership and Responsibilities

  • Student Organization Officer Guide - Information about leadership roles, expectations, and commitments.
  • Project Manager Guide - Guidance for students managing projects and team responsibilities.
  • Project Manager Commitment Form - A document outlining responsibilities for student project leads.

Ready to Get Started?

Once you have reflected on your goals, researched your project context, and discussed expectations with your team, you will be better prepared to begin collaborating with your project partner.

The next stage focuses on launching your project, establishing communication, and putting your preparation into practice.
