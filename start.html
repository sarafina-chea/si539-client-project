Starting Your Experience

Turning Preparation into Action

The beginning of an engaged learning project is an opportunity to establish a strong relationship with your community partner. During this phase, students begin professional communication, organize their first meetings, and clarify expectations for the work ahead.

Establishing clear communication and shared responsibilities early can help prevent misunderstandings and create a more productive working relationship.

Three Steps to Starting Your Project

Use these steps to move from initial contact to an organized project partnership.

1. Contact Your Community Partner

Begin by sending a professional introductory email to your client or community partner. Introduce your team, explain your connection to the project, and request an initial meeting.

According to the UMSI Initial Client Email Examples, your first email should include:

  • A professional greeting.
  • Your name and your team members' names.
  • An explanation of why you are contacting the partner.
  • Several possible meeting times and options for meeting.
  • An appreciative closing.

If possible, provide multiple scheduling options and ask which communication method works best for your partner.

View Initial Client Email Examples

2. Organize Your First Meeting

Your first meeting is an opportunity to learn about your partner, introduce your team, and begin defining the project.

The UMSI Initial Client Meeting Sample Agenda recommends covering six topics:

  1. Introductions: Share names, backgrounds, and relevant experiences.
  2. Organization and project: Learn about the organization, ask questions, clarify expected outcomes, and discuss privacy agreements when necessary.
  3. Timeline: Discuss the expected project completion date.
  4. Contacts: Identify primary and secondary contacts on both teams and verify their contact information.
  5. Communication: Agree on how and when to meet, communicate, and share updates.
  6. Final questions: Address remaining questions or uncertainties.

View Initial Client Meeting Sample Agenda

3. Establish Shared Expectations

After the initial meeting, make sure your team and partner understand the project's goals, responsibilities, communication practices, and expected outcomes.

For student organizations participating in the SO-ELL program, the Student Org-ELO Partnership Agreement outlines responsibilities for project engagement and communication.

  • Establish a regular communication schedule with the client.
  • Provide client status updates at least once per week.
  • Prepare an agenda at least 24 hours before each client meeting.
  • Respond to ELO or client emails within 48 hours.
  • Report project progress, challenges, and major issues to ELO as required.

These are requirements documented for SO-ELL student organizations. Students in other engaged learning programs should follow the expectations established for their own projects.

Building a Respectful Partnership

A successful engaged learning experience should consider both student learning goals and the needs of the community partner.

The Ginsberg Center's guiding questions encourage students to think about how community partners will participate in project planning, decisions, and evaluation.

Questions to Discuss with Your Partner

  • What does the community partner hope to gain from this project?
  • How will the partner contribute to defining project goals?
  • How will community priorities influence the project timeline and design?
  • How will the partner participate in decisions throughout the project?
  • What deliverables will be created, and how will the partner use them?
  • How will feedback be collected during the partnership?

Your Project Launch Checklist

Before moving into active project work, make sure you have completed these important starting tasks.

  1. Introduce your team to the community partner.
  2. Schedule and prepare an agenda for the first meeting.
  3. Discuss the organization's needs and the project's expected outcomes.
  4. Identify primary contacts and communication preferences.
  5. Clarify the project timeline, responsibilities, and deliverables.
  6. Discuss any necessary privacy or confidentiality agreements.
  7. Agree on how frequently the team and partner will communicate.
  8. Document decisions and remaining questions for follow-up.

Moving Forward

Once your team has contacted your partner, completed an initial meeting, and established shared expectations, you can begin moving into active project work.

Continue communicating regularly, documenting decisions, and checking that your work reflects the needs of your community partner.
